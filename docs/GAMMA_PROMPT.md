The interface calls the default baseline mode Adaptive and the congestion-reducing mode Dynamic. The Adaptive baseline currently uses fixed-timing signals; Dynamic uses responsive signal and routing rules. Distinguish these product names from the technical term adaptive control.

SyncRoute is a preventive traffic-management project for Indian cities. Its aim is to coordinate nearby intersections and route guidance before local queues spread into wider congestion. The current working prototype models Connaught Place, New Delhi.

The problem: fixed-time signals cannot respond well to uneven demand, festival surges such as Diwali, rain, incidents or emergency vehicles. A junction can release its queue into a road that is already full, moving the problem downstream. This wastes commuters' time, makes bus arrivals unreliable and delays emergency response. Better signal coordination helps, but cannot create unlimited road capacity or replace public transport and good urban planning.

Context: TomTom's 2025 data ranked Bengaluru second worldwide by congestion level, at 74.4%, with 168 hours lost annually under its standard rush-hour commuter model. State the year and metric; do not imply that every resident loses exactly that amount. Europe has Cooperative Intelligent Transport Systems and V2X deployments. India also has ATMS, incident detection, adaptive signals and priority systems; do not describe India's capabilities as CCTV-only. SyncRoute's opportunity is to combine local sensing, connected-junction decisions and route advice around measurable local needs.

The proposed approach uses H3, the geospatial indexing system developed at Uber, to organise traffic observations into hexagonal regions and compare neighbouring areas. H3 is a spatial index, not a communication protocol. Cellular/fibre/approved roadside networks would carry observations and commands. Actual directed roads and junctions determine legal routes and signal control; hexagonal boundaries do not replace road topology.

The proposed real-world system would combine consented vehicle speed/location observations, participating bus fleets and approved infrastructure sensors. It would estimate vehicle counts, density, queues, downstream storage and uncertainty, with weather and verified incident information. It must work with partial connected-vehicle coverage, including people who do not use an app.

The intended AI direction is cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning: one agent per signalised junction or approved junction group, centrally trained offline and executed locally. Agents observe queues, arrivals, downstream capacity, current phase, neighbouring conditions and verified priority requests. They can request approved phases or bounded green extensions. A city operations centre coordinates corridors and supervises the network. Independent safety rules enforce minimum green, amber/all-red clearance, pedestrian service, maximum waiting limits and blocked-exit restrictions. Safe fallback plans and operator override remain available.

Potential user benefits include more predictable journeys, congestion-aware route alternatives, conditional bus priority, authenticated emergency green corridors, protected pedestrian service, limits on residential diversion, and clearer incident response for operators. These are intended benefits that require evaluation, not proven field outcomes.

What exists now: a browser-based React/TypeScript/Vite prototype using MapLibre, H3 and bundled OpenStreetMap assets. It provides a detailed 3D city and simultaneous 2D overview, cars/motorbikes/buses/ambulances, fixed-versus-adaptive comparison using identical seeded demand and events, congestion colours, a transparent 30-second occupancy-trend projection, adaptive signals, preventive rerouting, local ambulance priority, decision logs, inspectors and JSON reports. Scenarios include full-city traffic, balanced demand, rush hour, a road incident and emergency response. It runs locally without external APIs after installation. Traffic calculations run in a worker to keep map interaction responsive.

Be precise: the running controller uses deterministic adaptive rules. No trained reinforcement-learning policy or live road-signal integration exists yet.
