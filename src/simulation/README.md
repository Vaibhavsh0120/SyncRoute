# Deterministic simulation rules

The manifest is generated from one seeded PRNG, frozen, and shared between worlds. Normal demand has 200 cars over 600 seconds; rush, incident and ambulance have 300 ordinary cars. The full-city preset is described separately below. Rush, incident and ambulance allocate exactly 65% of car origins/destinations to the Janpath–State Entry corridor (75% northbound). Fifty percent of cars adopt routing, assigned before the run. The ambulance is an extra trip at second 180. Both worlds run to second 1200, including a ten-minute drain period. Unfinished trips remain visible and included in conservation and waiting metrics.

The default city manifest instead has 900 ordinary vehicles across 600 seconds (90 per minute overall), plus the same emergency arrival at second 180. Ordinary classes are exactly 60% car, 30% bike and 10% bus. Origins cycle through every operational node; the first round is staggered across the first 60 seconds to cover the whole map. About 60% of trips choose an adjacent outgoing road destination and 40% choose a seeded random other operational node. The remaining arrivals fill seconds 60–599. This authored demand level allows both controllers to drain on the supplied road graph while retaining queues and emergency comparisons; it is not calibrated from measured city counts. Every class uses the same 7.5m logical single-lane storage slot; class labels are assumed display metadata, not a calibrated mixed-vehicle model. All vehicles belong to the engine and conservation ledger. There are no decorative traffic particles or external services.

Only city mode uses the authored weather timeline from `src/data/context.ts`: clear until second 119, light rain during 120–359 (movement speed multiplied by 0.85), then cloudy. Both controllers receive identical weather. Effective free-flow speed uses that same factor in vehicle and cell speed ratios, so rain alone does not create a false slowing warning. City mode also applies the capacity restriction during 180–419. The four earlier presets retain their original traffic/weather behavior for baseline comparisons. Internal vehicle kind, destination, planned route and environment timeline are assumptions; measured telemetry is limited to location and speed.

The engine steps in whole seconds. Vehicles move front to back on a single lane with 7.5m minimum separation. Link storage is the smaller of declared capacity and physical length divided by 7.5m. Crossing candidates are captured before any transfer, then processed deterministically with ambulance priority, waiting age and ID as tie-breakers. A receiving link must have storage and an entrance gap. Full boundaries retain departures outside the network. Discharge is at most one vehicle per incoming link per two seconds. The incident doubles that link's discharge headway from second 180 through 419; it does not destroy storage or vehicles. Simultaneous conflicting physical turning paths are abstracted to the supplied two-phase signal plan.

Fixed signals use seeded 20–40-second green durations, starting phases and offsets per junction. The small unit-test controller named `s` retains 30-second greens and zero offset. Every switch closes all entries for two seconds of amber and one second of all-red. Optimized controllers inspect competing demand every five seconds after a 10-second minimum green; 40 seconds is the normal maximum when another feasible approach has demand. Pressure combines stopped queue, arrivals within 30 seconds and emerging-congestion pressure, discounted by receiving storage. The leading vehicle determines single-lane feasibility: a follower's free turn cannot bypass a blocked head. An infeasible approach contributes no actionable pressure. A challenger needs more than 120% of current pressure; ties retain the green. A feasible competing demand approaching 90 seconds overrides score. A blocked receiver is excluded from that service target; physical gridlock cannot be promised a 90-second service. `serviceViolations` counts feasible delayed approach-seconds beyond the target.

Optimized ambulance priority detects the current incoming link within 120m of a signal, serves at least 10 seconds of the current green, requests its phase through normal clearance, holds its service while the ambulance approaches, and resumes the saved phase through clearance after at least 10 seconds of green. A blocked single-lane head suspends priority so feasible competing traffic can run; priority is requested again when downstream storage opens. No red or clearance entry is allowed. Ambulances cannot pass cars inside a single lane. Fixed controllers receive the same ambulance without preemption.

Occupancy is vehicle count divided by finite road storage. H3 resolution 10 receives each vehicle's exact current engine coordinate each tick. Display interpolation does not change H3 assignments. Cell storage divides link capacity by the fraction of sampled road length inside that cell (5m samples); cell occupancy is therefore road-storage occupancy, not covered land area. The past 30 seconds of per-tick occupancy are fitted by least-squares linear regression after at least five seconds of observations. Current occupancy plus 30 times that slope, clamped to [0,1], is the visible 30-second projection. This is a transparent local trend extrapolation, not a calibrated traffic prediction model.

RED means established congestion: at least three stopped vehicles and speed below 25% of free flow, or at least 85% occupancy with stopped traffic. YELLOW means a positive occupancy trend predicts at least 70% storage in 30 seconds, at least three vehicles slow below 70%, or stopped traffic is emerging. GREEN applies otherwise. Empty cells are green. A yellow cell propagates its warning to the roads with vehicles currently inside it, even if whole-link occupancy is still low. Yellow road risk increases proactive pressure and route cost before red conditions; prevention decisions expose when management becomes active. The controller consumes each tick's warnings on its next update. Cell and link categories can otherwise differ because their aggregation boundaries differ.

Optimized routing changes only at junctions, for the preassigned half of drivers, with a 30-second cooldown. A stopped link head can accept a qualifying diversion before signal feasibility is computed, including while red; it can cross only on green after clearance. Dijkstra costs include free-flow time, current queue discharge delay, a yellow warning penalty and near-full receiving storage. It respects directed connectivity and excludes an immediate U-turn. A diversion requires both 10 seconds and 20% predicted saving; it never teleports a vehicle or changes its current link. Optimized travel-time previews use the same costs; fixed previews retain the static free-flow path and estimate its travel time with current costs. Both are estimates, not realized trip times.

Waiting counts every boundary second plus on-road stopped seconds (speed below 0.5m/s). Reported speed preserves the distance actually travelled during the current tick: first reaching a red line or blocked receiver does not erase that movement or add a stopped second; the following motionless tick counts as stopped. Total system time accumulates requested unfinished vehicle-seconds; completed-trip mean and ambulance trip time include boundary delay from scheduled departure. Average speed is a 30-tick moving mean of on-road speed. Queue lengths and peaks are metres (stopped vehicles times 7.5). Spillback seconds are blocked incoming-link seconds. Histories sample every five seconds. Decision history retains the latest 100 with per-event cooldowns.
